Chapter 1a
Chapter 1 — The Neoclassical Growth Model

The Solow Model

Capital accumulation, steady states, and long-run growth dynamics.

Assumptions

Strong Assumptions

1
Constant returns to scale
2 ★
Decreasing marginal returns of inputs — the most important assumption.
3
Constant and exogenous saving rate in the economy
Key Results

Main Messages

Each economy converges toward a long-term steady-state path.
The steady states can be different from one country to another.
The further away the economy is from its steady state, the higher its growth rate.
Equation

Production Function

$$Y = F(K, L)$$
$Y$ — GDP
$K$ — stock of capital
$L$ — labour

Constant returns to scale

$$F(\lambda K, \lambda L) = \lambda F(K, L) \qquad \text{for } \lambda > 0$$

Positive and decreasing marginal returns

$$F_K > 0,\quad F_{KK} < 0,\quad F_L > 0,\quad F_{LL} < 0$$
$$\lim_{K\to\infty} F_K = 0, \qquad \lim_{K\to 0} F_K = \infty, \qquad \lim_{L\to\infty} F_L = 0, \qquad \lim_{L\to 0} F_L = \infty$$
Equation

Capital Accumulation

$$Y = C + I \qquad I = S$$

$I$ is the gross investment; $\dfrac{dK}{dt}$ is the net investment (net of capital depreciation).

$$\frac{dK}{dt} = sF(K,L) - \delta K$$
$s$ — saving rate (= net investment rate)
$\delta$ — capital depreciation rate
$n$ — population growth rate

In per-worker terms, letting $k = K/L$:

$$\frac{dk}{dt} = sf(k) - (\delta + n)k$$
Graph

The Solow Diagram

$sf(k)$ (concave) intersects $(\delta+n)k$ (linear) at the unique steady state $k^*$. Arrows show global convergence.
✎ Exercise 1
The Cobb-Douglas Case

In the case where $Y = K^\alpha L^{1-\alpha}$:

1.Show that when production factors are paid at their marginal returns, $\alpha$ is the share of capital income in GDP, and $1-\alpha$ the share of labour income.
2.Show that $f(k) = k^\alpha$ (where $k = K/L$).
3.What is the value of $k^*$ at which $dk/dt = 0$ ?
4.For this $k^*$, what is the growth rate of GDP per capita ?
5.For this $k^*$, what is the growth rate of GDP ?
6.Show that $s$ can be expressed in terms of $k^*$, $n$ and $\delta$.
7.Show that the growth rate of $k$ can be expressed in terms of $k/k^*$.
8.What is the growth rate of GDP per capita (out of steady state) ?
9.What is the growth rate of GDP (out of steady state) ?
1 — Capital and labour shares

The marginal product of capital is $F_K = \alpha K^{\alpha-1}L^{1-\alpha} = \alpha\,\dfrac{Y}{K}$.

Capital income $= F_K \cdot K = \alpha Y$, so its share in GDP is $\alpha$. Similarly, $F_L \cdot L = (1-\alpha)Y$, so labour's share is $1-\alpha$.

2 — Intensive form $f(k)$

Divide $Y = K^\alpha L^{1-\alpha}$ by $L$:

$$y = \frac{Y}{L} = \frac{K^\alpha L^{1-\alpha}}{L} = \left(\frac{K}{L}\right)^\alpha = k^\alpha$$

So $f(k) = k^\alpha$.

3 — Steady-state capital $k^*$

At $dk/dt = 0$: $s\,k^{*\alpha} = (\delta+n)k^*$, hence $k^{*\,\alpha-1} = \dfrac{\delta+n}{s}$, so:

$$k^* = \left(\frac{s}{\delta+n}\right)^{\!1/(1-\alpha)}$$
4 — Growth rate of GDP per capita at $k^*$

At the steady state, $k$ is constant, so $y^* = k^{*\alpha}$ is constant. The growth rate of GDP per capita is 0.

5 — Growth rate of GDP at $k^*$

$Y = yL$. Since $y$ is constant and $L$ grows at rate $n$, aggregate GDP grows at rate $n$.

6 — Saving rate in terms of $k^*$, $n$, $\delta$

From $s\,k^{*\alpha} = (\delta+n)k^*$:

$$s = (\delta+n)\,k^{*\,1-\alpha}$$
7 — Growth rate of $k$ in terms of $k/k^*$

The growth rate of $k$ is $\hat{k} \equiv \dfrac{\dot{k}}{k} = s k^{\alpha-1} - (\delta+n)$.

Substituting $s = (\delta+n)k^{*\,1-\alpha}$:

$$\hat{k} = (\delta+n)k^{*\,1-\alpha}\,k^{\alpha-1} - (\delta+n) = (\delta+n)\!\left[\left(\frac{k^*}{k}\right)^{\!1-\alpha} - 1\right]$$
8 — Growth rate of GDP per capita (out of steady state)

$y = k^\alpha$, so $\hat{y} = \alpha\,\hat{k}$:

$$\hat{y} = \alpha(\delta+n)\!\left[\left(\frac{k^*}{k}\right)^{\!1-\alpha} - 1\right]$$
9 — Growth rate of GDP (out of steady state)

$Y = yL$, so $g_Y = \hat{y} + n$:

$$g_Y = \alpha(\delta+n)\!\left[\left(\frac{k^*}{k}\right)^{\!1-\alpha} - 1\right] + n$$
Extension

Adding Total Factor Productivity

$$Y = (AL)^{1-\alpha}K^\alpha$$

where $A$ is Total Factor Productivity, growing at rate $g$: $$\frac{dA/dt}{A} = g$$

Redefine $k = K/(AL)$ (capital per efficient worker). The law of motion becomes:

$$\frac{dk}{dt} = sf(k) - (\delta + n + g)k$$
Population growth $n$ and TFP growth $g$ both dilute capital per efficient worker — hence $(\delta+n+g)$ replaces $(\delta+n)$.
✎ Exercise 2
The Cobb-Douglas Case with TFP

Answer the same questions as in Exercise 1 in this specific case, with $k = K/(AL)$ and $(\delta+n+g)$ replacing $(\delta+n)$.

2 — Intensive form

$Y = (AL)^{1-\alpha}K^\alpha$. Divide by $AL$:

$$\tilde{y} = \frac{Y}{AL} = \left(\frac{K}{AL}\right)^\alpha = k^\alpha \implies f(k)=k^\alpha$$
3 — Steady-state $k^*$

At $dk/dt=0$: $sk^{*\alpha} = (\delta+n+g)k^*$, so:

$$k^* = \left(\frac{s}{\delta+n+g}\right)^{\!1/(1-\alpha)}$$
4 & 5 — Growth rates at steady state

At $k^*$, $k = K/(AL)$ is constant. Since $A$ grows at $g$, $K/L$ grows at $g$, and $y = Y/L$ grows at $g$. So: growth rate of GDP per capita $= g$, growth rate of GDP $= n + g$.

6 — Saving rate in terms of $k^*$
$$s = (\delta+n+g)\,k^{*\,1-\alpha}$$
7 — Growth rate of $k$ in terms of $k/k^*$
$$\hat{k} = (\delta+n+g)\!\left[\left(\frac{k^*}{k}\right)^{\!1-\alpha} - 1\right]$$
8 & 9 — Growth rates out of steady state

Growth rate of GDP per capita: $\hat{y} = g + \alpha\,\hat{k} = g + \alpha(\delta+n+g)\!\left[\left(\dfrac{k^*}{k}\right)^{\!1-\alpha}-1\right]$

Growth rate of GDP: $g_Y = \hat{y} + n$

Golden Rule 1/2

The Saving Rate that Maximises Consumption

Is there a saving rate $s$ that maximises consumption per efficient worker at the steady state?

$s$ can be expressed in terms of $k^*$:

$$s = (\delta+n+g)\,k^{*\,1-\alpha}$$

Consumption per efficient worker at steady state:

$$c = (1-s)\,k^{*\alpha} = k^{*\alpha} - (\delta+n+g)\,k^*$$

Maximising over $k^*$, this is maximum for:

$$k^{**} = \left(\frac{\alpha}{\delta+n+g}\right)^{\!1/(1-\alpha)} \qquad \text{which is the case if } s = \alpha$$
The Golden Rule: When the saving rate equals the share of capital income in GDP ($s = \alpha$), the steady state is the one where consumption per efficient worker is maximum.
✎ Golden Rule 2/2 — Questions
A higher saving rate is not necessarily better

Note that a higher saving rate is not necessarily better for the economy. Consider:

1. An economy where the golden rule is not respected and $s > \alpha$. What would happen to the per capita consumption of all generations if this economy enforced the golden rule saving rate?
2. What if, on the opposite, the saving rate is $s < \alpha$?
1 — Case $s > \alpha$ (over-saving)

If $s > \alpha$, the economy is at a steady state $k^*$ that is above the golden rule level $k^{**}$. Consumption per efficient worker is below its maximum.

Enforcing $s = \alpha$ reduces saving, which increases consumption immediately (since less is saved) and also raises steady-state consumption. Every generation benefits: current consumption rises on impact and the new steady-state consumption is higher. This transition is Pareto improving.

2 — Case $s < \alpha$ (under-saving)

If $s < \alpha$, the economy is below the golden rule: $k^* < k^{**}$. Enforcing $s = \alpha$ requires increasing saving, which reduces current consumption on impact. Although the long-run consumption is higher, current and near-term generations must sacrifice. This is not Pareto improving — there is a trade-off between generations.
